⚠️ Reality Check

A2 → B2 normally takes ~300–400 hours. Full-time mode gives you ~10 h/day × 24 days ≈ 250 h. That's close enough to make it a real fight.

It's an internal Erasmus exam, not Goethe. Internal exams are usually shorter and check whether you can function: talk about yourself and your studies, write a clear text, understand normal speech, and get the grammar mostly right. That's good news. A clean B1+ with B2 connectors often passes.

Biggest unknown: the format. Until you know it, the plan trains all 4 skills + grammar equally. On Monday Oct 12, get the format (see 🕵️ Exam Intel) and re-weight.

Second risk: burnout. Nobody does 10 h of deep work a day. The day mixes ~6 h of hard blocks with ~4 h of lighter input. Respect the breaks and the weekly half-day off, or you lose days 15–24.

Why this exists
Grammar is a puzzle for you; vocab isn't. So turn vocab into rules. Your C2 English decodes the Germanic core, your Spanish decodes the Latin layer (Information, Universität, Situation, Diskussion). Anki only what these rules can't decode.
Rule 1 · Gender by suffix (free articles)
die: -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion, -tät, -ik, -ie, -enz/-anz (Freiheit, Meinung, Lösung)
das: -chen, -lein, -ment, -um, -nis (mostly), nominalized verbs (das Lernen)
der: -ling, -ismus, -or, -er for people/tools (der Lehrer, der Computer)
Rule 2 · Word-building suffixes
-weise ≈ -mente: glücklicherweise, beispielsweise
-bar ≈ -able: machbar, trinkbar · -los ≈ -less / sin: arbeitslos, sinnlos
-lich / -ig ≈ -ly / -y: freundlich, wichtig
Rule 3 · Consonant shift (EN → DE)
t → z/s/ss: two → zwei, water → Wasser, ten → zehn
p → f/pf: apple → Apfel, sleep → schlafen, open → offen
d → t: day → Tag, door → Tür, dance → tanzen
th → d: think → denken, thing → Ding, three → drei
Rule 4 · Prefix = direction (≈ phrasal verbs)
auf ≈ up/open (aufgeben = give up, aufmachen = open) · zu = closed (zumachen)
an / aus = on / off-out (anmachen; ausmachen = switch off / agree on; ausgehen = go out)
ab ≈ off/away (abschneiden, absagen = cancel) · ein ≈ in/into (einkaufen, einschlafen)
um = change/around (umziehen = move house) · vor = fore/pre (vorbereiten, vorstellen)
ver- often = wrong/away/changed state (vergessen, verlieren, sich verlaufen = get lost) · er- = reach a result (erreichen, erfahren)
Rule 5 · Compounds are descriptions
Last part = meaning + gender: Hand + Schuh = der Handschuh, kühlen + Schrank = der Kühlschrank. Split long words before you look them up.
Reading
Read the questions first, then scan for them. Never translate whole texts in the exam. Paraphrase hunt: the correct answer rarely reuses the question's words (Arbeit → Beruf, schnell → rasch). Identical words in text and option are often the trap. If a statement sounds logical but isn't actually in the text, it's wrong. Stuck >90 s → guess and move. Never leave a blank.
Listening
Use the pause before each audio to underline key words in the questions. Speakers often mention several options; the real answer often comes right after aber / jedoch / trotzdem / eigentlich. Mark during the first play, confirm on the replay.
Post-mortem (after every practice part)
For every wrong answer write why: vocab / grammar / trap / time. Anki the vocab ones. The most frequent "why" decides your next fix-it block.
