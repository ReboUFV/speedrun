Today's blocks

    German in the margins

    This is where most of your hours come from. No desk, no effort: you already do these things in Spanish or English.

    • Meals and cooking: a podcast or a Mediathek documentary.
    • Walks, gym, commute: yesterday's listening clips, or a new episode.
    • Evening: a series or film with German subtitles. Don't pause to study.
    • Phone, computer and YouTube in German; search in German.
    • Bed: ten pages of a novel, only marking what you don't get.

    The loop

    Input you enjoy → mine the sentences you almost understood → write or say something short every day → get it corrected → the corrections become cards. Everything else on this site feeds that loop.

    Rules

    1. Volume of native input beats everything else.Read and listen to things Germans consume, at a difficulty where you get 80–90% and the rest is guessable. Documentaries, news, podcasts, novels, Mediathek. Most of your hours are here, and most are in the margins.
    2. Mine from what you actually met.Cards come from your own reading and listening, as whole sentences with the target hidden. A frequency list does the opposite: words you may never meet. Ten new cards a day is plenty.
    3. Output every day, with correction.Ten minutes, five to eight sentences, written or spoken. Claude corrects it, you keep the corrections as cards. It is the cheapest feedback you can get, and it is what stops errors from fossilising.
    4. Make German necessary.Move one part of your real life into German: the phone, your notes, a group chat, a hobby community, a language exchange partner. Study alone doesn't create the pull that daily use does.
    5. Grammar on demand.Don't work through the book in order. Take the mistake that repeats in your corrections, learn that rule in German, test it, move on. At this level, the B2–C1 book is a reference, not a course.
    6. At C1, vocabulary is collocations and register.Not more nouns: verbs with prepositions, connectors, nominal style, idioms, and the words of the field you care about. The Prompts tab has the tools for it.
    7. Shadow short clips.Take 30–60 seconds of audio you understand, then repeat it with the speaker, at their speed. It trains the speed of your ear and your mouth together. Learners swear by it; the evidence is anecdotal.
    8. Rest is part of the plan.Light days keep the habit alive. A missed day costs nothing; a month of guilt costs a lot. There is no streak to protect.

    How long it really takes

    Commercial sources put C1 at about 600–900 hours from zero for a Spanish speaker, and the US FSI at about 800 class hours for English speakers. Self-reported learner logs vary a lot: one LingQ learner reported around 350 hours of listening and several million words read for C1-level reading.

    My estimate, not a source: if you are at B2 now, expect roughly 250–450 more hours of real work for C1. At 2–3 hours a day, counting the margins, that is about 3 to 6 months. The date moves with the hours, so don't fix one.

    Evidence quality: mostly forum posts, blogs and course pages. Treat the numbers as ranges.

    Self-checks, no logging

    Do one when it feels like time, about monthly. If you pass it twice in a row, make it harder.

    • Reading: a 1,200-word Zeit or Spiegel article in 12 minutes, then a spoken summary with few gaps.
    • Listening: a 5-minute Tagesschau or DW report without subtitles; you can retell most of it.
    • Speaking: 20 minutes with a native speaker without switching to English.
    • Writing: 200 words on an opinion topic with at most three errors Claude can find.
    • The exam itself: a Goethe C1 mock paper (free on goethe.de) when you feel ready.

    Pace, no deadline

    • Total new cards: about 25 a day. New words 10, Paráfrasis 8, Sustantivos 5, Präpositionen 5, Verbos frecuentes 2 or 0.
    • Reviews come first. With over 150 due, stop adding new cards until it's under 100.
    • The big decks come in priority order. You don't need to finish them; stop one when the cards stop being new to you.
    • On a Light day: reviews and nothing else.

    Mining format

    Note type Respuesta anidada (Cloze). Text: the full sentence with the target as {{c1::...}}. Back Extra: a short German definition and where you met it. Add the audio clip when you have it. The Mine a text prompt writes these lines for you.
