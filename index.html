Make a unit solid

  1. First pass (day 0, 45–60 min). Skim the theory for 2 minutes, type every exercise, check after each one. Log the %.
  2. Mine vocabulary (10 min). 5 to 8 items from the unit's sentences in the Mine vocab tab: verbs with preposition and case, phrases, connectors, general nouns.
  3. Make it yours (10 min). Write 5 sentences with the structure about your own life, using 3 of the words you just mined. Check them with the "5 own sentences" prompt.
  4. Repeat pass (day +2). Only the exercises you got wrong. No theory. Log it.
  5. Check (day +5). The 10-item "Unit check" prompt, cold. 80% or more makes it solid.
  6. Use it. Put the structure into tonight's five shutdown sentences on purpose.

80%+ moves to the next step (a first pass at 80%+ skips straight to the check). 60–79% repeats in 2 days. Under 60% starts the failure protocol.

When a unit scores under 60%

  1. Freeze the topic. No harder unit of the same topic until this one is fixed. Other topics continue.
  2. Diagnose (5 min). Sort mistakes into form (endings, conjugation), position (word order) or choice (wrong case or structure). Fix the biggest pile only.
  3. Go one level down. A red B2–C1 unit sends you to its A1–B1 sibling first (shown on the unit). A red A1–B1 unit: write the rule in one line next to Spanish and English, plus a mini table.
  4. Targeted drill (20 min). The "Failure drill" prompt with minimal pairs until 8 in a row are right.
  5. Retry next day. Only the failed exercises. 60% or more returns it to the normal cycle.
  6. Still under 60%? Park it. Max about 3 hours per unit. In the exam, say it with a structure you know.

Listening Priority 1

In the exam. Use every pause to read the questions and underline the key words. Predict the answer before it plays. Mark on the first play, confirm on the second. The real answer often comes after aber, jedoch, eigentlich, trotzdem; what's said first often gets corrected. Watch numbers, times and negation (kein, nicht, nie). Never leave a blank.

Daily method. Questions first, play twice, score it. Then the transcript: find the exact words behind each answer and why you missed them (speed, a word, a trap, a paraphrase). Finish with 10 minutes of shadowing.

Material. Goethe-Zertifikat B1 and B2 model tests (free on goethe.de, with audio and answers), DW Langsam gesprochene Nachrichten and Top-Thema (with transcripts), Easy German with subtitles. Move from slow to normal speed through the week.

Reading Priority 2

In the exam. Questions first, then scan. The right answer is usually a paraphrase (Arbeit → Beruf); options that copy the text's words are often the trap. Not in the text means wrong. Stuck over 90 seconds: guess and move on.

Daily method. Two texts against the clock. For each wrong answer, quote the sentence that held it. Words that cost you an answer go to Mine vocab.

Material. Goethe B1 and B2 model tests (Lesen), DW Top-Thema, nachrichtenleicht.de, and the "Reading practice" prompt.

Grammar

Expect multiple choice, gap fill or a C-Test. The points are in verb position (V2, and the verb at the end after dass, weil, obwohl), case after prepositions and verbs, adjective endings, connectors, the passive, Konjunktiv II and relative pronouns.

In the exam. Read the whole sentence before choosing. Find what sets the case (a preposition or a verb) and where the verb has to go. Don't change an answer unless a rule backs the change.

Daily. New units at 09:00, a 30-item drill at 11:00, repeats and a C-Test at 17:00. Your error patterns are on the Diagnosis page.

Decode instead of memorizing

Gender by suffix. die: -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion, -tät, -ik, -ie. das: -chen, -lein, -ment, -um, -nis, verbs as nouns. der: -ling, -ismus, -or, -er for people and tools.

Suffixes. -weise is -mente (glücklicherweise). -bar is -able (machbar). -los is -less (arbeitslos). -lich and -ig are -ly and -y.

Sound shift from English. t to z/s (two, zwei). p to f/pf (apple, Apfel). d to t (day, Tag). th to d (think, denken).

Prefixes are directions. auf up/open, zu closed, an/aus on/off, ab off/away, ein in, um change, vor before, ver- wrong or changed, er- result.

Compounds. The last part gives meaning and gender: Hand + Schuh = der Handschuh.

Why the plan looks like this

One exam in three parts: grammar, reading and listening. The score sets the level. There's no writing or speaking, so every hour goes to understanding German and getting the forms right.

Listening first, then reading. They're your weakest parts, and they improve fastest with daily timed practice and post-mortems. Grammar comes easily to you: the units keep it climbing and the drills turn it into points.

Vocabulary carries both: most wrong answers in reading and listening come from a word you didn't know or a paraphrase you didn't spot.

The remaining risk is burnout, so keep the off blocks.
